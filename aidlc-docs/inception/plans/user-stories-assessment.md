# User Stories assessment — Draw & Dash

**Decision.** Execute User Stories at standard depth. The team approved the [requirements](../requirements/requirements.md) and requested this stage. The [clarification record](../requirements/requirement-verification-questions.md) supplies the agreed player journey and scope.

## Why stories are useful here

- [x] **Direct player impact.** A casual player can start with a sketch, a description, or a voice recording. A text-only player expects a generated sprite, while a player who supplies a drawing expects that drawing on the track. Stories can make both outcomes visible in review (FR-01–FR-03).
- [x] **Business rules with observable consequences.** A dog may stop for food and then chase the rival who passed it. The player should be able to recognize both habits, use a single cheer, and understand the result even if the dog loses (FR-04–FR-05, FR-08).
- [x] **Several parts of the experience must agree.** During an overtake, the player sees movement, reads a caption, and may hear the narrator. A story can specify what happens when a line becomes stale, the player mutes, or the race finishes (FR-06–FR-09, NFR-01–NFR-02).

## Expected outcome

The team will be able to review each player goal against an observable example and trace each requirement to a story or a constraint on that story. The casual player is the primary actor. The course team can verify scenarios using fresh inputs and prepared examples. The planned persona and story artifacts will be generated after plan approval.
