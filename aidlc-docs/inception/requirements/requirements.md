# Draw & Dash requirements

**Status:** Approved by Group 22 on November 24, 2026, at the AI-DLC v1.0.1 Requirements Analysis gate.

## Intent and scope

Group 22 requested a standalone casual web game in which a player invents a pet and recognizes its appearance and habits during a race. This is a new greenfield project spanning multiple components: creation and AI interpretation, a local race simulation, and event-based text and speech. Complexity is moderate because asynchronous AI output and audio must stay aligned with a short, autonomous race. Standard-depth analysis is appropriate for a single local player and a fixed course.

The December 16 demo must support a full round with either fresh inputs or team-prepared examples. The November 24 milestone covers Inception through Units Generation. These requirements follow the team-approved [README](../../../README.md), [vision](../../../vision.md), [technical environment](../../../technical-environment.md), and the team decisions in the [requirements clarification record](requirement-verification-questions.md).

## Functional requirements

| ID | Required behavior | What the team can review in the demo |
|---|---|---|
| FR-01 | Create a pet from a drawing, typed text, or recorded speech. Each input works independently and inputs can be combined. Offer a stroke eraser, Clear Canvas, an editable transcript, and prepared examples. | A first-time player can reach pet review using each input on its own. Erasing one stroke preserves the others. Clearing the canvas removes the drawing. A prepared example enters the same review and race flow. |
| FR-02 | Interpret appearance and habits, show the proposed pet card, let the player correct it, and ask for clarification when inputs conflict. The player confirms the interpretation before racing. | For a winged doodle described as unable to fly, ask what the wings mean. When the player confirms they are decorative, retain the wings and assign behavior consistent with that answer. |
| FR-03 | Use the player's original drawing as the racing sprite whenever provided. Otherwise generate a static sprite in a simple hand-drawn style from the confirmed appearance. Preserve approved artwork on a habit-only correction and reuse it for replay. | Compare a drawing-only, text-only, and voice-only racer. The drawing is recognizable as the player's own work. Each of the other racers displays artwork derived from its description. |
| FR-04 | Race one human-created pet against two computer pets on one fixed course in approximately 30 seconds. The race runs autonomously. The human player can cheer once per race. The engine applies consistent movement rules to all three pets and determines finish order. | Let one race finish without cheering. In another race, use the cheer and attempt it again. Only the first cheer can affect performance. |
| FR-05 | Support up to two special habits per pet. Execute movement effects sequentially, using reusable actions such as pausing, slowing, or bursting. Show the effect through the static sprite, track objects, and visual cues. A habit may cost a pet its lead. | A food-loving dog dips toward a snack and pauses. If its chase habit is eligible when it resumes, the player sees it accelerate after a rival. A cat can visibly scratch a post. A teammate can recognize the distraction with captions hidden and sound muted. |
| FR-06 | Generate an opening introduction and routine AI lines before racing. Display a routine line when its event occurs. Generate selected highlights during play from the current race log and standings. Keep the simulation running while text is generated. | A routine snack caption appears when the dog stops. A live highlight can describe a recorded overtake. Discard a late line whose facts have been superseded or whose race has ended. |
| FR-07 | Offer browser speech synthesis with a persistent Sound on / Mute control. Speak the opening and eligible in-race lines. Speak a routine line when the narrator is free and a selected live highlight while its event is current. Allow one speaking line and at most one pending highlight. | During a race with sound on, the player hears the snack call, recognizes the dog dipping toward the treat, and mutes the line. Speech and the speech queue stop immediately while racers and captions continue. Unmuting picks up the next eligible line. |
| FR-08 | Display the official engine ranking at the finish, clear race chatter, and provide a brief AI post-race analysis of how habits and player actions affected performance. Show the recap and read it when sound is enabled. Offer replay with the confirmed pet. | If the log shows the cat passing the dog while it ate, the recap may report that pass. The rankings agree with the engine. Starting another race clears prior narration and allows one new cheer. |
| FR-09 | If a required API operation fails, identify the failed operation and offer restart. When the device lacks a usable speech voice, keep captions available and display sound status. | Simulate a failed sprite request. The player receives a creation error and can restart. A restarted race cannot play an earlier race's pending line. |

## Quality requirements and constraints

| ID | Constraint | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| NFR-01 | The engine owns the official event ordering and ranking. Confirmed pet traits, the course, identical timed inputs, and the same seed if randomness is used must reproduce the race result. | Compare event order and final ranking in repeated simulations. Check commentary claims against the log separately, allowing the wording to vary. |
| NFR-02 | Race feedback must stay timely and legible. Captions remain available with speech muted. Replace a pending highlight with a fresher or higher-priority one when appropriate. | Exercise overlapping events, mute/unmute, finish, and restart. Check that speech never overlaps, stale highlights are discarded, and narration does not delay the ranking. Verify audible playback on the demo laptop. |
| NFR-03 | Run locally in a browser with a loopback Express server and internet access for OpenAI. Use the approved vanilla TypeScript, Vite, HTML5 Canvas, Node.js, and npm environment. | Exercise a full round on a team laptop with one player-created pet and two bundled computer pets. Use the versions and integration boundaries in the [technical environment](../../../technical-environment.md). |
| NFR-04 | Keep credentials on the server and player data in session memory. Disclose OpenAI processing before submission, request microphone permission when recording starts, stop recording tracks afterward, enforce input limits and formats, and validate model output before applying effects. Exclude raw inputs and generated content from logs. | Inspect client-visible configuration and logs. Try an oversized upload and an unsupported model-proposed effect. End the session and check that player submissions are discarded locally. OpenAI retention is governed separately by provider policy. |
| NFR-05 | Plan for OpenAI `gpt-6.1-terra` for interpretation and commentary, `gpt-image-2.6-flare` for sprites without drawings, and `gpt-transcribe` for voice. Use browser speech synthesis for playback. The initial API budget is $20, including $5 reserved for rehearsal and the final demo. | Use mocks for routine development and automated tests. Reuse sprites and bundle computer pets. Kelan will verify access, latency, and actual cost on the team account. The estimate allows up to two live highlight calls per race. |
| NFR-06 | Use example-based tests and partial property-based testing for core game logic and data transformations. Use Vitest and fast-check for pure rules and Playwright for browser journeys. | Exercise the one-cheer limit, zero through two habits, reproducible rankings, and round-trips for lossless transformations using generated domain inputs. Keep failing seeds and shrunk cases reproducible. Browser checks cover independent and combined inputs, clarification, preservation of artwork, sound, recap, and error recovery. |

## Scope boundaries and open decisions

Extra courses and same-device multiplayer are optional after the core game is complete. Online multiplayer, accounts, a pet database, persistent training or companion care, a tool-calling AI race director, hosted voice generation, and per-pet skeletal or video animation are outside the MVP.

The initial habit catalog, trigger tie-breaks, effect magnitudes and durations, and cheer timing during a pause remain open for design. So do the threshold for a live highlight, its freshness window, the precise finish-to-recap cutoff, and final recording and upload limits. Target-device microphone capture and audible playback, sprite wait times, model access, actual charges, and any switch to `gpt-6-luna` for commentary require later evidence.

## Extension decisions

Resiliency Baseline is off. Security Baseline is off, with the team's security requirements retained in NFR-04. Property-Based Testing is partial. NFR-06 records the intended properties and framework. Rules PBT-02, PBT-03, PBT-07, PBT-08, and PBT-09 apply in their designated later stages. Requirements Analysis has no implementation-level PBT check.
